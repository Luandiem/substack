# WRITING GUIDE

Version: 4.0

---

# PART I
# EDITORIAL DNA

## Purpose

The editorial identity of **Luận Điểm | Business Research** is built upon one simple belief:

> Investors make better decisions when they understand businesses rather than simply following stock prices.

Every article should educate, explain and encourage independent thinking.

The objective is not to predict the market.

The objective is to improve the reader's understanding of businesses, industries and long-term value creation.

---

## Editorial Identity

Luận Điểm is not a news platform.

It is not a stock recommendation service.

It is not a market commentary website.

Luận Điểm is a long-form business research publication.

Its purpose is to transform financial information into business understanding.

Every article should leave readers with deeper insight rather than stronger emotions.

---

## Editorial Values

Everything published by Luận Điểm should be:

- Objective
- Evidence-based
- Educational
- Analytical
- Independent
- Long-term oriented
- Easy to understand
- Professionally written

Knowledge is more important than prediction.

Understanding is more valuable than certainty.

---

## Editorial Voice

The editorial voice should always feel:

Professional without sounding academic.

Confident without arrogance.

Analytical without being complicated.

Educational without becoming a textbook.

Thoughtful without being speculative.

Calm without becoming boring.

Readers should feel that the article is written by a trusted researcher rather than a market commentator.

---

## Editorial Objective

Every article should help readers answer three questions:

What happened?

Why did it happen?

Why does it matter over the long term?

If these three questions are answered well, readers can build their own investment conclusions.

---

# PART II

The mission of Luận Điểm is simple:

Help Vietnamese investors understand businesses before making investment decisions.

Rather than providing recommendations, Luận Điểm provides analytical frameworks.

Rather than predicting prices, Luận Điểm explains value creation.

Rather than following market sentiment, Luận Điểm focuses on business fundamentals.

---

## Long-term Mission

Create one of Vietnam's most trusted business research libraries.

Every article should remain valuable months or even years after publication.

Timeless knowledge is preferred over temporary market commentary.

---

## Educational Mission

Every article should teach readers something new.

Examples include:

- Understanding a business model.
- Reading financial statements.
- Evaluating competitive advantages.
- Assessing management quality.
- Understanding valuation assumptions.
- Identifying long-term risks.

Learning should be the primary outcome.

---

## Reader Mission

Readers should gradually become able to:

Read annual reports independently.

Interpret financial statements.

Recognize business quality.

Separate facts from opinions.

Evaluate management objectively.

# PART III
# EDITORIAL PHILOSOPHY

Everything written by Luận Điểm follows these principles.

---

## Business Before Stock Price

Understand the business before discussing valuation.

Understand valuation before discussing investment attractiveness.

Stock prices are outcomes.

Businesses are the causes.

---

## Research Before Opinion

Opinions are only meaningful when supported by evidence.

Every important conclusion should be supported by:

- Financial performance
- Industry context
- Management decisions
- Competitive positioning
- Reliable sources

Opinion should never replace research.

---

## Evidence Before Conclusion

Never begin with conclusions.

Build the argument step by step.

Observation

↓

Explanation

↓

Analysis

↓

Evaluation

↓

Conclusion

Readers should understand how conclusions are reached.

---

## Context Before Numbers

Financial numbers rarely explain themselves.

Every important metric should answer:

Why did it change?

Is the change temporary or structural?

What should investors continue monitoring?

Context creates meaning.

---

## Quality Before Growth

Growth alone is insufficient.

Always ask:

Is growth profitable?

Is growth sustainable?

Can the company allocate capital efficiently?

Can competitive advantages strengthen over time?

Long-term quality creates long-term growth.

---

## Long-term Thinking

Ignore short-term market noise whenever possible.

Focus on:

Business quality.

Competitive advantages.

Capital allocation.

Cash generation.

Management execution.

These determine long-term value creation.

---

## Independent Thinking

Every article should encourage readers to think independently.

Present both opportunities and risks.

State assumptions clearly.

Recognize uncertainty.

Readers should develop their own conclusions.

# PART IV
# THE FOUR MASTERS DNA

Luận Điểm does not imitate any individual author.

Instead, it combines the strongest principles from four complementary schools of investment thinking.

The result should always sound like Luận Điểm—not like any single writer.

---

## Morgan Housel
### The Storyteller

Morgan Housel reminds us that investing is ultimately about people, behavior and decision-making.

From this philosophy, Luận Điểm adopts:

- Begin with curiosity.
- Explain difficult ideas simply.
- Use storytelling before statistics.
- Make readers think instead of forcing conclusions.
- Connect business concepts to real-world experiences.
- Write naturally and conversationally.

Readers should remember the idea behind the numbers.

Never use storytelling merely for entertainment.

Every story should improve understanding.

---

## William J. O'Neil
### The Growth Analyst

William J. O'Neil emphasizes measurable business execution.

From this philosophy, Luận Điểm adopts:

- Focus on quarterly execution.
- Analyze revenue growth.
- Analyze EPS growth.
- Evaluate profitability.
- Study return on equity.
- Understand business catalysts.
- Explain operational momentum.

Growth should never be presented without explaining its underlying drivers.

Financial performance should always be linked back to the business.

---

## Compounding Quality
### The Business Quality Mindset

Compounding Quality focuses on businesses capable of creating value over decades.

From this philosophy, Luận Điểm adopts:

- Durable competitive advantages.
- Capital allocation discipline.
- Pricing power.
- Cash generation.
- High-quality management.
- Long-term value creation.
- Business resilience.

Every article should ask:

Can this business become stronger over the next decade?

Business quality should always come before valuation.

---

## Aswath Damodaran
### The Valuation Thinker

Damodaran reminds us that every valuation depends on assumptions.

From this philosophy, Luận Điểm adopts:

- Separate business quality from investment attractiveness.
- Explain valuation assumptions.
- Recognize uncertainty.
- Compare multiple scenarios.
- Avoid false precision.
- Present valuation as a range rather than certainty.

A wonderful business is not automatically a wonderful investment.

Readers should understand both quality and price before forming conclusions.

---

## The Luận Điểm Approach

Every article should naturally combine these four perspectives.

Storytelling attracts attention.

Business analysis creates understanding.

Financial analysis explains performance.

Business quality evaluates durability.

Valuation provides context.

Risk analysis restores balance.

The final result should be objective, educational and evidence-based.

Readers should never feel that the article promotes a stock.

Instead, they should feel that they understand the business better than before reading.

# PART VI
# WRITING PRINCIPLES

Every article published by Luận Điểm should follow these principles.

---

## Start with Curiosity

Begin with:

- an interesting question
- a business challenge
- an industry trend
- a strategic decision
- an unexpected observation

Avoid beginning with:

- stock prices
- valuation ratios
- quarterly earnings
- conclusions

Curiosity keeps readers engaged.

---

## Teach Before Persuading

The purpose of writing is education.

Help readers understand:

- How the business works.
- Why financial performance changed.
- What creates competitive advantages.
- What determines long-term success.

Never attempt to convince readers to buy or sell.

---

## Explain Before Concluding

Every important conclusion should be supported by explanation.

Follow this sequence:

Observation

↓

Explanation

↓

Evaluation

↓

Conclusion

Readers should understand the reasoning behind every conclusion.

---

## Business Before Numbers

Financial statements tell the results.

Businesses explain the reasons.

Always explain:

- products
- customers
- business model
- competitive position
- industry dynamics

before discussing financial performance.

---

## Evidence Before Opinion

Support every important statement with evidence.

Possible evidence includes:

- Financial statements
- Annual reports
- Investor presentations
- Industry reports
- Official announcements

Never replace evidence with speculation.

---

## Balance Before Bias

Every business has strengths.

Every business has weaknesses.

Discuss both.

Avoid confirmation bias.

Readers deserve balanced analysis.

---

## Independent Thinking

Do not tell readers what to think.

Instead provide:

- information
- context
- assumptions
- analysis

Readers should develop their own conclusions.

---

## Long-term Perspective

Avoid focusing on short-term market fluctuations.

Instead discuss:

- competitive advantages
- business quality
- industry evolution
- management execution
- long-term value creation

Time is one of the most important variables in business analysis.

# PART IX
FINANCIAL WRITING

Financial statements are not the story.

They are evidence that supports the business story.

Every financial discussion should answer three questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. Why does it matter?

Never report numbers without interpretation.

Always connect financial performance to business operations.

---

## Financial Priorities

Prioritize analysis in the following order:

1. Revenue Growth
2. Gross Profit
3. Operating Profit
4. Net Profit
5. Earnings Per Share (EPS)
6. Gross Margin
7. Operating Margin
8. Net Margin
9. Return on Equity (ROE)
10. Return on Assets (ROA)
11. Operating Cash Flow
12. Free Cash Flow
13. Capital Expenditure
14. Balance Sheet Strength
15. Debt Profile

Financial metrics should explain business quality rather than simply describe accounting results.

---

## Trend Analysis

Always compare:

• Quarter-over-Quarter (QoQ)

• Year-over-Year (YoY)

• Three-Year Trend

• Five-Year Trend (when available)

A single reporting period rarely tells the complete story.

Long-term trends are generally more informative than short-term fluctuations.

---

## Quality of Earnings

Not all earnings are equal.

Evaluate whether earnings are supported by:

- Revenue growth
- Margin improvement
- Operating leverage
- Cash flow
- Sustainable business drivers

Avoid treating accounting profit as business performance.

---

## Cash Flow

Cash flow reveals business reality.

Always explain:

- Operating Cash Flow
- Free Cash Flow
- Cash Conversion
- Working Capital Changes

Strong accounting profit without cash generation deserves further investigation.

---

## Explain Before Concluding

Instead of writing:

"Revenue increased 18%."

Explain:

Revenue increased because the company expanded distribution, introduced higher-value products and benefited from stronger customer demand.

Business explanation should always precede numerical interpretation.

---

# PART X
GROWTH ANALYSIS

Growth should never be treated as an isolated number.

The purpose is to understand **why growth occurs** and **whether it can continue**.

---

## Sources of Growth

Identify where growth comes from.

Possible drivers include:

- Higher sales volume
- Better pricing power
- New products
- Geographic expansion
- Market share gains
- Capacity expansion
- Industry recovery
- Acquisitions
- Digital transformation

Growth without identifiable drivers is incomplete analysis.

---

## Growth Quality

Evaluate whether growth is:

Structural

or

Cyclical.

Structural growth usually creates long-term value.

Cyclical growth may reverse when industry conditions change.

Always distinguish between the two.

---

## Sustainable Growth

Growth should be evaluated alongside:

- Profitability
- Cash generation
- Return on capital
- Competitive advantage

Rapid growth that weakens business quality should be discussed objectively.

---

## Future Growth

Separate:

Confirmed growth drivers

from

Expected growth drivers.

Clearly identify assumptions.

Never present expectations as facts.

---

# PART XI
COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE WRITING

Competitive advantage explains why a business can outperform competitors over long periods.

This section should focus on business economics rather than marketing claims.

---

## Areas to Evaluate

Consider:

- Brand strength
- Distribution network
- Cost advantage
- Technology
- Customer loyalty
- Switching costs
- Network effects
- Regulatory licenses
- Economies of scale

Not every company possesses a durable competitive advantage.

State this clearly when appropriate.

---

## Competitive Position

Discuss:

- Market leadership
- Industry ranking
- Market share trends
- Competitive intensity
- New entrants
- Substitute products

Always evaluate the competitive landscape, not just the company itself.

---

## Durability

Ask:

Can this advantage remain relevant over the next decade?

Temporary advantages should not be treated as economic moats.

---

## Evidence

Support every claim with evidence.

Avoid vague statements such as:

"The company has a strong brand."

Instead explain:

Why the brand is valuable.

How it influences customer behaviour.

How it improves pricing power.

How it supports profitability.

---

# PART XII
CAPITAL ALLOCATION WRITING

Capital allocation is one of the strongest indicators of management quality.

Great businesses create value not only by earning profits, but by allocating capital effectively.

---

## Evaluate Management Decisions

Discuss:

- Capital Expenditure
- Research & Development
- Mergers & Acquisitions
- Dividends
- Share Buybacks
- ESOP
- Debt Financing

Explain why each decision matters.

---

## Long-Term Value Creation

Always ask:

Does this decision increase long-term shareholder value?

Short-term earnings improvements should never be confused with long-term value creation.

---

## Capital Efficiency

Evaluate:

- Return on Invested Capital
- Return on Equity
- Asset Utilisation
- Cash Generation

Capital efficiency often matters more than business size.

---

## Management Discipline

Strong management usually demonstrates:

- Consistent strategy
- Rational investment decisions
- Conservative leverage
- Long-term thinking

Judge management by actions rather than promises.

---

# PART XIII
VALUATION WRITING

Valuation is an exercise in probability rather than certainty.

Separate valuation from business quality.

A wonderful business can still be an expensive investment.

---

## Purpose

The objective of valuation is not to predict future prices.

The objective is to understand the expectations already reflected in today's valuation.

---

## Valuation Methods

Select methods appropriate for the business.

Examples include:

- Price to Earnings (P/E)
- Price to Book (P/B)
- EV / EBITDA
- Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)
- Comparable Companies
- Historical Valuation Range

Explain why each method is appropriate.

---

## Assumptions

Every valuation depends on assumptions.

Clearly explain:

- Revenue growth
- Profit margins
- Discount rate
- Terminal growth
- Industry outlook

Readers should understand how assumptions influence conclusions.

---

## Scenario Analysis

Whenever possible, discuss:

Base Case

Bull Case

Bear Case

Different scenarios help readers appreciate uncertainty.

---

## Valuation Discipline

Never state:

"The stock is worth..."

Instead write:

"Under these assumptions, the current valuation appears to reflect..."

This reinforces analytical discipline rather than false precision.

---

## Final Principle

Business quality and valuation are complementary but independent analyses.

A great business is not automatically a great investment.

Likewise, a cheap valuation does not guarantee an attractive opportunity.

Always discuss both separately before forming an investment thesis.
# PART XIV
RISK WRITING

---

## Purpose

Every investment thesis is incomplete without understanding its risks.

The purpose of the Risk section is not to discourage investment, but to help readers understand what could invalidate the thesis and what indicators deserve continuous monitoring.

Risk analysis should be balanced, evidence-based and forward-looking.

---

## Risk Philosophy

Every business has risks.

Outstanding companies are not risk-free.

Instead, they understand, manage and adapt to risks better than their competitors.

The objective is to identify:

- Material risks
- Probability
- Potential impact
- Monitoring indicators
- Management's ability to respond

---

## Categories of Risk

Consider risks from multiple perspectives.

### Business Risk

- Demand slowdown
- Customer concentration
- Product disruption
- Supply chain dependency

---

### Industry Risk

- Industry cycle
- Competitive intensity
- Technological disruption
- New entrants

---

### Financial Risk

- Debt
- Liquidity
- Interest rates
- Foreign exchange exposure

---

### Regulatory Risk

- Government policy
- Tax changes
- Licensing
- Environmental regulations

---

### Execution Risk

- Expansion
- New projects
- M&A integration
- Digital transformation

---

### Management Risk

- Capital allocation
- Governance
- Succession planning
- Incentive structure

---

## Writing Principles

Avoid simply listing risks.

Instead explain:

- Why the risk matters.
- How it affects the business.
- Whether the risk is temporary or structural.
- What investors should monitor.

---

## Risk vs Uncertainty

Not everything unknown is a risk.

Clearly distinguish:

Known risks

vs

Unknown uncertainties

Explain both whenever appropriate.

---

# PART XV
FACT VS OPINION

---

## Purpose

One of Luận Điểm's core values is intellectual honesty.

Readers should always know:

What is objectively known.

What is reasonably inferred.

What remains uncertain.

---

## Facts

Facts are supported by verifiable evidence.

Examples include:

- Financial statements
- Annual reports
- Investor presentations
- Official announcements
- Government statistics

Facts should never be exaggerated.

---

## Opinions

Opinions are logical interpretations based on available evidence.

Opinions should:

- Explain assumptions.
- Remain balanced.
- Recognize uncertainty.
- Avoid certainty when evidence is incomplete.

---

## Assumptions

Some conclusions require assumptions.

Always explain:

- Why the assumption is reasonable.
- What evidence supports it.
- What could invalidate it.

Readers should clearly understand the difference between assumptions and evidence.

---

## Forecasts

Forecasts are scenarios rather than predictions.

When discussing the future:

Avoid certainty.

Prefer:

- May
- Could
- Likely
- Potentially

Avoid:

- Will
- Definitely
- Guaranteed

---

## Editorial Integrity

When evidence is insufficient:

Say so.

Never invent information.

Never force conclusions.

Intellectual honesty is more valuable than appearing confident.
